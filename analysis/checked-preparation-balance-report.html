Hi Adam,

The current fight is not yet well suited to being the first enemy: the 8-Health rat is quite lethal, and checked Preparation usually offers too little value for its lost turn.

I tested the current published rat encounter using 340,000 headless fights through the production combat coordinator, plus an independently reviewed probability model. All 40 simulated strategy/scenario rates agree with the model within 1.89 standard errors. The current engine suite also passes all 2,804 tests.

The baseline is player Health 10, Energy 3, Agility 3, Composure 3, versus rat Health 8. Health and Energy cap at 10. Player attacks check Agility; Preparation checks Composure; both use difficulty 3, giving 62.5% success at a stat of 3. Player Defend still costs 1 Energy and rolls fixed 3d6 ≥ 10 only after a rat attack hits. Rat attack/block checks are fixed at the same 62.5%; the rat has no stats. Hidden intentions and the existing turn order are preserved.

Preparation is the weakest payoff. At baseline, optimal survival is 74.64%; forbidding Preparation entirely still allows 74.45%. Its best possible contribution is only 0.19 percentage points. For the six-Health rat the contribution is even smaller, 0.04 points. The tested cue-based preparation strategy wins 70.4%, below always attacking at 73.2%; repeatedly preparing until a bonus is available wins only 56.9%.

The reason is the double risk: spend a turn to attempt Preparation, then risk missing or being blocked on the following Attack, which consumes the bonus. As a simple illustration starting unprepared, with no enemy defense or interruptions, Preparation followed by Attack averages 2.03 damage over two actions; two normal attacks average 2.50. This illustration is not a dominance proof, but it explains the poor general payoff. Failed Preparation preserves any bonus already banked.

Increasing Agility from 3 to 4 improves optimal survival by 11.58 points; increasing Composure from 3 to 4 improves it by only 0.47 points. Composure currently helps only Preparation, while Agility helps every attack. Defense does not use Composure in this version.

More Energy is not the main fix. Starting with Energy 10 rather than 3 raises optimal survival against the eight-Health rat only from 74.64% to 74.80% (+0.16 points). More Energy can even hurt a fixed policy that keeps defending too often, because it delays killing the rat and creates more exposure. An optimal policy can always ignore the surplus. The model enforces Energy costs and affordability; it does not assign a future value to unused Energy.

First-enemy assessment. Even optimal play loses 25.4% of current eight-Health fights. Reducing rat Health to 6 improves optimal survival to 89.3%, but always attacking already reaches 88.8%, so that change mainly improves forgiveness rather than tactical depth. At player starting Health 8 and 6 against the current eight-Health rat, optimal survival falls to 55.7% and 33.9%, respectively (model-only sensitivity checks).

Recommended next steps.

  1. Keep enemy intentions hidden, as requested. The immediate problem is action payoff, not the lack of revealed intentions.
  2. Keep Preparation checked if that is the intended design, but test a stronger reward or allow a prepared bonus to survive a missed attack. Test one change at a time; neither proposal has been validated by this run.
  3. Use a separate six-Health rat as the first-enemy tuning baseline, then evaluate incoming damage and the actual starting resources. Aim for forgiving outcomes under simple sensible play, not only under an optimal policy.
  4. Evaluate retained Health, Energy spent, duration and a sequence of encounters with the intended recovery rules. Win probability alone does not measure the campaign value of saving Energy.

Limits. These are fair independent digital-dice tests. They do not validate physical dice fairness, mobile/browser animation, usability, or real beginner decisions. Optimal survival is a mathematical upper benchmark for an informed player, not a prediction of player behavior. No game rules, saved projects, or published versions were changed during this balance test.
