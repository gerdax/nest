# The Nest first five chapter scenarios

Draft for story development. Lazarus wakes with no memory in an underground complex. His immediate purpose is to survive and get out. Each chapter advances that attempt while giving him enough evidence to ask better questions about the place and himself.

The five chapters below adapt the Entrance, Security, Reception, Engine Room, and Surface Vehicles Garage material. Their locations and historical threads come from existing lore; Lazarus’s arrival, the escape arc, and their arrangement into chapters are proposals. A chapter can span several rooms or parts of a floor. Thirty two floors remain a working background structure.

## Starting assumptions

- The hero is Lazarus, but whether he knows that name at the start is undecided. The scenarios do not require him to know it.
- He has total autobiographical amnesia. For this draft, he retains language, ordinary knowledge, and practical abilities needed to act. He does not know the current date, where he came from, or why he is here.
- He wakes in darkness beneath a distant opening. A beam of light reaches down from very high above. A fall is a possible explanation, not an established event.
- The opening is unreachable. The first accessible route leads deeper; later, evidence of other exits gives him a reason to continue descending.
- He begins without a narrated history of the world. The player learns alongside him.
- The game uses situation and action cards. Required story moments establish the chapter’s arc; encounters between them vary within controlled pools and can react to earlier choices.
- Memory flashes are sparse, brief, and incomplete. They do not explain the bunker or prove a connection to its past.

## Shape of the opening arc

| Chapter | Immediate objective | Main discovery | Reason to continue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Awakening | Find light and a route out of the landing area | Other people were trapped here | A service route leads down |
| 2 Security | Find access through the sealed checkpoint | The complex was deliberately locked down during an emergency | A route to reception becomes accessible |
| 3 Reception | Learn where the exits and controls are | This was a large working community, not just a shelter | Machinery below may control a surface route |
| 4 Engine Room | Restore access to the vehicle garage | Someone already tried to overcome the lockdown | The garage can be reached |
| 5 Vehicle Garage | Use the surface transport route to escape | That route is unusable, and the danger is still alive | A deeper route offers the next credible lead |

By the end of Chapter 5, Lazarus has a direction and a reason to investigate. He has not learned what caused the disaster, how much time has passed, or who he is.

## Chapter 1 Awakening

### General scenario

Lazarus regains consciousness on a hard floor. He can make out a distant patch of light above him, but little around him. He checks his body and possessions and discovers that he cannot remember his name or how he arrived. Let the player establish this through immediate observations and actions, without a long internal monologue.

The landing area opens into what appears to be the entrance of an underground facility. The scale is difficult to judge in the darkness. A huge sealed door and a crowd of bodies suggest that people gathered here trying to leave. Their condition is disturbing, but Lazarus has no reliable sense of their age.

His first success is small: securing a usable light source and finding a way beyond the entrance hall. Getting out of this room means descending.

### Required story moments

1. Wake beneath the distant opening and establish the missing memory.
2. Obtain enough light to explore. A working flashlight is the proposed means; alternate searches converge on this essential access rather than allowing a failed search to stop the story.
3. Find the sealed main door and evidence of a failed evacuation.
4. Discover Ada in a locker and recover her short recorded warning. It mentions lockdown, danger below, and Oskar’s attempt to reach the door machinery.
5. Clear the route to the service stairs, adapting the existing forklift obstruction and key discovery.

### Variable encounters and choices

Search luggage, a security booth, damaged storage, or abandoned clothing. Let players choose between gathering supplies, examining an unsettling detail, and moving quickly. Possible consequences include a minor injury, useful equipment, or a clue that can be revisited later.

Examining bodies should have a narrative purpose. One may hold a key; another shows evidence of violence near the exit. Do not make every corpse a source of loot.

### Climax and transition

A sound or movement interrupts the final search. Lazarus cannot yet identify its source. He opens the stair route and leaves the bright patch far behind.

The main discovery is that he is not the first person who wanted to escape. Ada’s warning supplies the first human voice from the past, without explaining that past. No memory flash is needed here.

## Chapter 2 Security

### General scenario

The stairs lead into the security area. It is more functional and confined than the entrance: guard quarters, lockers, surveillance equipment, and a sealed access door. Some systems still have power. Lazarus hopes to find someone alive, a map, or a way to release the main door.

Instead, he finds an emergency response abandoned halfway through. Guards were ordered to the infirmary. Barricades face inward. The threat appears to have come from inside the complex.

### Required story moments

1. Find a security terminal or recorded dispatch directing personnel to the infirmary.
2. Establish that lockdown controls more than one door and cannot be cancelled from this checkpoint.
3. Find the officer’s quarters and obtain an access card, adapting Lieutenant Mark Schmidt’s room.
4. Encounter a security hazard: a malfunctioning turret is a suitable source-based proposal.
5. Use the card to reach the route toward reception.

### Variable encounters and choices

Search guard lockers, inspect surveillance, cross an improvised barricade, or investigate a noise in an empty room. The turret can be avoided, distracted, or disabled through an available environmental interaction. Required progress must remain possible without finding an optional weapon.

Choices can affect injuries, supplies, and whether Lazarus leaves a noisy or quieter route behind him. Surveillance should show fragments of panic rather than a complete recording of the disaster.

### Climax and transition

Lazarus crosses the active security zone and opens the sealed checkpoint. He now knows the confinement was deliberate. He does not yet know whether lockdown was an act of protection, cruelty, or desperation.

Reception promises clearer information: people normally entering this place must have been told how to navigate it.

## Chapter 3 Reception

### General scenario

Reception is unexpectedly grand. Comfortable seating, a front desk, decorative architecture, and a holographic greeter suggest an institution that expected visitors and valued its own importance. Ordinary messages reveal people working shifts, waiting for deliveries, and worrying about relatives.

This chapter puts everyday life beside the horror. The first unmistakable mutated human encounter belongs here, adapting the existing reception scene. Its human clothing matters: whatever happened affected the people who lived and worked in the facility.

### Required story moments

1. Enter the lobby and encounter the first clearly changed inhabitant.
2. Find the front desk messages, including concern about aggressive behaviour elsewhere and a conversation between Erica and Walter Heitmeyer.
3. Restore or access part of the reception information system.
4. Learn the facility’s name, The Nest, and locate the vehicle garage and its machinery access.
5. Obtain the maintenance access needed to descend, adapting the technician’s body and unusual shaft key.

### Variable encounters and choices

Explore the IT room, courier luggage, reception storage, or an adjacent service area. Decide whether to restore the greeter for information despite the sound it may create. Observe, evade, or confront the changed inhabitant, with consequences suited to the eventual encounter mechanics.

The restored system provides selected useful information. It should not deliver a full institutional history or solve the lockdown. Nazi insignia and German text can be visible evidence; Lazarus’s familiarity with their meaning remains undecided.

### Climax and transition

Lazarus secures maintenance access while the changed inhabitant threatens the lobby. The information he has recovered points to machinery below that serves a vehicle lift to the surface.

He leaves with his first plausible escape plan. He also has evidence that this place once held ordinary people, some of whom tried to help each other.

## Chapter 4 Engine Room

### General scenario

The service route descends into machinery that operates the main doors, lifts, and garage transport. Lazarus is now pursuing a specific plan: reach the vehicle garage and use its surface connection.

He discovers the person Ada mentioned. Oskar died while attempting to intervene in the machinery. His unfinished letter to Ada gives their story an intimate ending without explaining the whole emergency. Their actions also help Lazarus understand which systems might still be usable.

### Required story moments

1. Reach the lift machinery and identify the garage access system.
2. Find Oskar’s body and his letter to Ada. Use Oskar consistently in this draft; the older Otto naming remains a source inconsistency.
3. Discover that releasing the main vault door requires more than a local repair.
4. Restore or bypass enough of the garage access mechanism to continue.
5. Open the route into the garage.

### Variable encounters and choices

Explore maintenance stores, ventilation passages, an emergency supply box, and an old internal newspaper. The newspaper can establish that the facility was operational in 1959, without telling Lazarus the present year or the age of the bodies.

Offer repair approaches with different costs: spend useful material, take a more dangerous manual route, or search for a safer workaround. Each valid route achieves garage access. Optional supplies change the cost, not whether the chapter can be completed.

### Proposed memory flash

During a manual operation, a sound or tactile sensation triggers a very brief fragment: a hand resting on his, or a voice saying “Wait.” The fragment cuts off before he can place the person or setting.

This is the first possible flash, not a required clue. It raises the possibility that some memory survives. It must not establish that he worked in The Nest or caused its disaster.

### Climax and transition

The mechanism moves, with enough noise to suggest Lazarus has disturbed something nearby. He passes through the newly opened access.

He has accomplished what Oskar could not finish, but Ada and Oskar’s fate gives his escape attempt greater weight. The garage is now a concrete hope rather than a label on a map.

## Chapter 5 Vehicle Garage

### General scenario

Military vehicles, transport trucks, and civilian luggage fill the garage. A dead family in a vehicle makes the failed evacuation visible at a personal scale. Lazarus searches for the surface lift and believes he may finally be near an exit.

The lift itself can respond, but its route above is physically blocked or damaged beyond what he can repair. This is a proposed replacement for the old surface camp and crater arrangement. Chapter 4 opened access to the garage; it did not guarantee that the entire surface transport route survived.

### Required story moments

1. Enter the garage and locate the surface lift controls.
2. Discover the family’s vehicle and signs of an interrupted evacuation.
3. Attempt to operate the surface lift and establish why escape through it is currently impossible.
4. Face an active changed inhabitant while resolving the failed attempt.
5. Find a credible deeper lead before leaving: proposed maintenance signage points to another transport connection elsewhere in the complex, with a nearer route through the missile station.

### Variable encounters and choices

Search vehicles and a workshop, investigate transport records, or choose a route through obstructed parking lanes. A vehicle alarm or powered machinery can attract danger if activated. Supplies and information gathered earlier can alter the available responses.

Oskar’s garage record connects the dead mechanic to his workplace. Other records can suggest that the complex extends much farther than Lazarus has explored, without displaying all thirty two floors as a list of future chapters.

### Climax and transition

The attempted escape fails while danger closes in. Lazarus survives by using the garage environment and reaches the route deeper into the facility. The encounter can end through evasion, containment, or confrontation; the next chapter does not depend on a particular combat result.

He has lost his first escape plan but gained another direction. His objective remains getting out. Learning what happened here is increasingly useful to that objective, and his own missing past has become a second unresolved thread.

## Information and pacing boundaries

At the end of these chapters, Lazarus can know the facility’s name, that it was a military and research institution, that an emergency led to lockdown, and that some inhabitants changed violently. He has witnessed unusual preservation without receiving an explanation for it.

Keep the following discoveries for later chapters:

- The present year and the two centuries since the disaster.
- The Last Conflict and the extent of destruction outside.
- The entropy anomaly, its origin, and its relationship to mutation.
- Riedel’s research and reasons for activating lockdown.
- The general’s identity, motives, and role in the missile launches.
- Emma’s location and circumstances.
- Lazarus’s identity, arrival, and any connection to the complex.

Required discoveries stay in the authored chapter path. Variable encounters may reinforce them or add detail, but must not reveal their explanation early. A clue acquired in one chapter can alter a later response without requiring every player to find every optional record.

## Decisions to settle before writing individual cards

1. Does Lazarus know his name, discover it on an object, or acquire it later?
2. What does he have when he wakes, and what does his appearance suggest without proving?
3. What prevents the changed inhabitants from leaving the complex? The distant opening makes this relevant even though the answer can be revealed later.
4. What is the actual alternative escape route? Chapter 5 needs a truthful partial lead, even if the journey proves more complicated.
5. Should the first memory flash occur in Chapter 4, later, or in response to an optional discovery?
6. Where does Anton now belong? His existing surface trader role needs a new place in Lazarus’s story. He is not placed in these chapters yet.
7. When should Emma first make contact? The existing material introduces her around Recon Offices; that remains a useful later placement.
